# Datasets used by the benchmark

Raw data is **not committed** (`data/raw/` is git-ignored). Re-download with:

```bash
python scripts/fetch_datasets.py          # needs a (free) Kaggle API token in ~/.kaggle/kaggle.json
python scripts/build_manifest.py          # -> data/manifest.csv (person-level splits)
```

| Name in manifest | Source (Kaggle mirror) | Content used | Licence / terms | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ubiris_v2` | `chinmoyslg/ubirisv2` (mirror of UBIRIS.v2, Proença et al., Univ. Beira Interior) | NICE.I subset images (400x300 RGB, visible light, at-a-distance, *noisy* by design) + IRISSEG-EP "OperatorA" noise-free iris masks (Hofbauer et al., ICPR 2014) | UBIRIS.v2 is distributed by its authors **for research only** after a signed request; the Kaggle mirror lists the licence as "Unknown" | Results are **research evaluation only** and not evidence for a commercial release. For a product, request UBIRIS.v2 from the authors or (better) collect consented A-EYE phone images. |
| `pupil_eye_iris` | `itguides/pupil-eye-and-iris-segmentation` | Eye/iris/pupil masks (see build_manifest.py for the class mapping) | Listed as MIT on Kaggle; provenance of the underlying images is not documented | Used as a **second domain** to test cross-dataset generalisation |

## Derived labels committed to this repo

* `data/labels/color_labels.csv` – iris colour appearance labels (blue / brown / green_mixed / uncertain) assigned by
  visually inspecting iris crops (contact sheets generated by `python -m bench.color --sheet`).
  One annotator, no adjudication: treat as a **pilot** label set, not ground truth. The A-EYE
  annotation manual (research doc §6.1) should replace it with two-reviewer labels.

## What these datasets can and cannot tell us

* They **can** benchmark capture-quality metrics, iris/pupil localisation, normalisation and
  colour measurement on real visible-light eye images.
* They **cannot** benchmark the six SRS marking categories: no public dataset labels lacunae,
  white markings, collarette subtypes, contraction furrows or fibre types in iridology terms.
  Marking detectors are therefore evaluated by *synthetic injection* into real iris textures
  (`bench/markings.py`) – this measures detectability of a marking of known contrast/size, not
  agreement with a practitioner.
* UBIRIS.v2 images were captured with a Canon EOS 5D at 4–8 m, not a phone held at
  10–20 cm. A-EYE phone photos will differ (resolution of iris texture, lighting, glare), so
  every threshold must be re-calibrated on A-EYE data.
